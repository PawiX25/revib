The PlayStation game, rebuilt from its decompiled code. Put your own disc in: choose the disc image of Vib-Ribbon (PAL), its .cue file together with all of its .bin files.

The files stay on this computer. You can also drop them here.

Your music

Choose the songs to play to. They become the tracks of a CD, in the order you choose them, and the game builds a course from each one as it plays.

MP3, FLAC, WAV, OGG or anything else your browser plays.

The game wants its own disc back

Your music comes out of the drive and the Vib-Ribbon disc goes back in.

Picture

The game draws everything with straight lines. Sharp draws those lines again at your screen's full resolution, so they stay thin and smooth at any size. PlayStation shows the console's own 512 × 288 picture, enlarged, with its jagged edges.

The same moment of the first Bronze course, drawn both ways. Drag across it to move the divider.

Music

On the console the songs did not come straight from the disc to the speakers. The game read them into the sound chip's memory and played them from there, in mono at 22 kHz. CD plays the disc's own stereo recording at 44.1 kHz instead, in time with the game.

Sounds

Vibri's voice and every sound effect are short samples that the sound chip plays at different pitches. Bright fills in between their samples in straight lines and keeps the high notes. PlayStation uses the chip's own smoothing filter: softer and duller, exactly as the console sounded.

Controls

In the menus, click what you want, turn the mouse wheel to move through them, and right-click to go back.

The keys stand in for the PlayStation's buttons. To change one, click it and press the new key; Delete clears it.

Esc always goes back.

Gamepads work too, with the PlayStation's layout.
